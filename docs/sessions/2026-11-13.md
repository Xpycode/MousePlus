### Confirmed code findings and limits

- `PermissionsService` polls during setup and stops after a grant transition. It exposes
  `onGranted`, with no corresponding runtime permission-loss callback/shutdown path.
- `TriggerOnboardingLaunch` records permission grants; no revocation path resets runtime state.
- The November 10 `MouseButtonTriggerMonitor` active session tap consumes matched auxiliary
  down/drag/up events without checking trust again. Disabled-tap notifications cause unconditional
  re-enabling. It has no permission-loss cancellation path.
- HUD dismissal uses separate global/local NSEvent monitors. No permission-loss safeguard closes
  the panel and stops trigger interception if the dismissal mechanism becomes unavailable.
- The ring uses a floating nonactivating NSPanel, stays visible on deactivation, and hide orders
  it out then releases references. Inspect focus, tracking and teardown behavior further.
- These findings establish missing safeguards. They DO NOT establish the exact cause of broad
  input unresponsiveness, prove which OS event paths stopped, or prove a WindowServer/TCC bug.
  The mouse tap mask covers auxiliary buttons, not all clicks/keyboard events. Preserve that
  distinction; do not claim the tap directly swallowed every device's input.
- The five prior headless tests used a fake OS tap. They tested routing/consumption, not real
  permission revocation, OS focus recovery or global input delivery. The assistant's previous
  manual revocation instruction lacked a verified safe shutdown path.
